# Paper and Dataset Inventory

**Reviewed:** 2026-10-18
**Implementation status updated:** 2026-10-21
**Project:** Trustworthy AI-Assisted Coral Reef Health Assessment
**Review basis:** Direct review of all 19 PDFs under `papers/` and `papers/archive/`, plus the Phase I Review I presentation at [`BTP_Phase I_Review I_Presentation _Group_9.pptx.pdf`](../BTP%20artifacts/BTP_Phase%20I_Review%20I_Presentation%20_Group_9.pptx.pdf). This inventory records the papers' tasks, datasets, reported results, fit to the current implementation, and differences that prevent direct metric comparison. It does not use earlier literature-selection notes as the basis for paper selection.

## Current implementation and scope

The current code uses CoralscapesV2 and one 7-class project taxonomy across YOLO26n-sem, YOLO26n-seg, SegFormer-B5, and DINOv3-DPT. It derives benthic composition and visible coral-condition indicators, checks image quality and backend-specific prediction signals, and aggregates sampled video frames. The legacy V1 workflow and separate ResNet18 crop classifier have been removed. See [`main.py`](../main.py), [`src/inference/pipeline.py`](../src/inference/pipeline.py), [`src/evaluate/segmentation.py`](../src/evaluate/segmentation.py), and [`src/trust/reliability.py`](../src/trust/reliability.py).

The current four-backend evaluation is recorded in [`reports/model_comparison.md`](../reports/model_comparison.md) and [`reports/model_comparison.json`](../reports/model_comparison.json). It evaluates the 473-image CoralscapesV2 test split using the project 7-class mapping. YOLO26n-sem reaches **51.94% mIoU**, YOLO26n-seg **41.04%**, SegFormer-B5 **67.87%**, and DINOv3-DPT **72.65%**. These are project-taxonomy scores; they are not the dataset authors' 39-class benchmark results.

The comparison evaluates segmentation and derived health outputs; it is not a calibration, OOD, expert-cover, or external-site validation study. The video path samples frames and aggregates frame reports; it does not create a 3D reconstruction, measure a metric-width transect, or evaluate repeat-survey consistency. The current repository does not implement the presentation's full temporal change-detection plan or Lakshadweep-specific validation. Its predictions are an image-analysis prototype, not validated ecological measurements.

## Recommended references for the current report

For the **implemented model**, lead with CoralscapesV2; the project benchmark is reported separately from the original Coralscapes paper’s 39-class results. For the **current ecological output**, González-Rivero is the closest precedent for aggregating image annotations into cover/composition and checking against experts. For the **sampled-video path**, Ouassine is a useful presentation-selected reference, but its colony tracking is not implemented; DeepReefMap is the closer whole-survey video-to-cover workflow, and CoralVOS is the closer continuous video-segmentation benchmark. Kopecky supports bleaching-related outcomes. Use Wyatt's Safe AI and uncertainty-aware papers for the distinct trust goals of detecting distribution shift and propagating uncertainty into ecological estimates. Shao et al. provide condition-recognition context; Dey et al. supports Lakshadweep ecological interpretation and future local validation.

In short: **closest to what runs now: CoralscapesV2; closest to the presentation's intended baseline: Ouassine; closest to the broader video-to-cover system goal: DeepReefMap.** These answer different notions of “closest” and should not be collapsed into one ranking.

Report metrics by task. Do not compare pixel accuracy, mIoU, instance mask mAP, tracking scores, expert-cover error, OOD AUROC, and survey repeatability as though they measure the same quantity. A defensible direct Coralscapes benchmark still requires matching the published split, labels, mask protocol, ignored-pixel rules, and scoring implementation.
