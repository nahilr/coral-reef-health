# Core paper comparison with the current implementation

> Archived after consolidation. The authoritative paper ranking and implementation comparison is in [paper-inventory.md](../paper-inventory.md).

Checked 2026-10-18. This note compares the papers named in `archive/core-papers-selection.md` with the current code and saved reports. Paper metrics are not interchangeable: the project papers evaluate field ecology, point-label classification, dense segmentation, survey repeatability, out-of-distribution (OOD) detection, or cover-estimation error.

## Current implementation baseline

- Data: Coralscapes, 2,075 images. Official split counts are 1,517 train, 166 validation, 392 test.
- Segmentation: YOLO26n-seg trained for 30 epochs on 512-pixel inputs; the 39 original labels are collapsed to seven broad classes.
- Reported metrics: test mIoU 0.4038 at confidence 0.20; per-class IoU is 0.5181 live coral, 0.2427 bleached coral, 0.1269 dead coral, 0.3663 algae, 0.2657 rubble, 0.7903 sand/rock, and 0.5168 other. Validation mask mAP50 is 0.1652.
- Calibration report: ECE 0.375 from 7,598 detection-level samples. The reported 0.7437 accuracy is whether a detection class matches the majority ground-truth class inside its predicted mask; it is not pixel accuracy.
- Secondary ResNet18: crop-level validation accuracy 0.3822, macro F1 0.2264, balanced accuracy 0.3373 on 382 crops.
- Inference reports frame-level cover and review flags. The video path samples frames and averages accepted-frame estimates; it does not build a metric 3D transect map or compare repeat surveys.

Sources: [`reports/segmentation_metrics.json`](../../reports/segmentation_metrics.json), [`reports/training_run.json`](../../reports/training_run.json), [`src/evaluate/segmentation.py`](../../src/evaluate/segmentation.py), [`src/inference/pipeline.py`](../../src/inference/pipeline.py).

## Foundation papers and distance from the implementation

| Paper | What it contributes and its reported result | How close the implementation is |
|---|---|---|
| Dey et al. (2025), [Lakshadweep decadal coral composition](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/ddi.70043) | Ecological foundation, not an ML benchmark. Uses 24 years of observations at 12 sites across Agatti, Kadmat, and Kavaratti; examines 29 coral genera alongside depth, wave exposure, bleaching history, and recovery. Coral cover declined by about half; recovery was slower early after disturbances and faster around years 6–7. | **Contextual link only.** Current code has no Lakshadweep training/test data, site/depth/exposure covariates, genus-level analysis, or historical trend model. Its generic reef-health tiers do not reproduce Dey et al.'s ecological analysis. No direct accuracy comparison applies. |
| González-Rivero et al. (2020), [AI-assisted reef monitoring](https://www.mdpi.com/2072-4292/12/3/489) | Automated image annotation aggregated into benthic abundance and compared with expert observations across monitoring regions. Reports 97% unbiased agreement, 4% overall error, and 83–94% agreement for community composition across bioregions; reports at least 200× faster analysis at about 1% of manual cost. | **Closest ecological aim, limited validation match.** The implementation estimates composition/cover from image predictions, but it has no expert-agreement, abundance-error, repeatability, time, or cost benchmark. Its mIoU measures dense pixel overlap, so it cannot be compared directly with the paper's agreement/error numbers. |
| Sauder et al. (2025), [Rapid consistent reef surveys with DeepReefMap](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-025-20795-z) | Video-to-3D semantic transect mapping, tested on 365 video replicates at 45 Red Sea transect sites. Reports mean per-site/per-time standard deviation of live-coral cover of 2.51%. The work also evaluates held-out image segmentation and finds domain-specific training data can improve mIoU more than simply doubling generic training data. | **Workflow inspiration, not a reproduction.** This project samples video frames and averages estimates, but has no 3D mapping, transect-line detection, metric-width cropping, repeat replicates, or survey-level repeatability evaluation. The paper's 2.51% repeatability SD is not comparable to this project's mIoU. |
| Wyatt et al. (2025), [Safe AI for coral reefs](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S157495412500216X) | Benchmarks OOD detectors across Rowley Shoals, Catlin Pacific, and Moorea surveys. Uses AUROC and FPR@95%TPR for sample-level OOD plus histogram intersection for survey-level OOD. KNN-distance was the most consistent method, followed by ViM; confidence-only methods were weaker. | **Motivation is present; core method/evaluation is absent.** The project gates on quality heuristics, confidence, coverage, and entropy, but has no held-out geographic/survey shift test or OOD AUROC/FPR/HI. Its ECE is not an OOD-detection metric. |
| Wyatt et al. (2026), [Uncertainty-aware coral monitoring](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ecoinf.2026.103831) | Studies calibrated probabilities and Monte Carlo sampling for estimating benthic abundance on Rowley Shoals, Moorea, and Catlin data. Its main outcome is cover/abundance error and propagated uncertainty, not one headline classification accuracy. The paper reports that unbalanced training plus Monte Carlo calibrated predictions reduces cover error; class balancing can cause errors above 20 percentage points in some cases. | **Only a conceptual link today.** The current code uses deterministic mask counts and entropy over YOLO detection scores. It does not calibrate class probabilities, Monte Carlo-sample predictions, or propagate uncertainty into cover intervals. Its ECE and classifier accuracy do not measure the paper's cover-estimation error. |

## Dataset paper and the nearest metric comparison

Sauder et al. (2025), [The Coralscapes Dataset](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/ICCV2025W/CVAUI%20%26%20AAMV../../papers/Sauder_The_Coralscapes_Dataset_Semantic_Scene_Understanding_in_Coral_Reefs_ICCVW_2025_paper.pdf), is the direct data/segmentation reference. It contains 2,075 images and 39 classes, with a spatial site split of 1,517/166/392. Its benchmark repository reports test mIoU of 55.031 for SegFormer MiT-b5 and 57.800 for a stronger-augmentation MiT-b5 model; the listed test pixel accuracies are 80.939 and 82.761, respectively ([authors' benchmark results](https://github.com/eceo-epfl/coralscapesScripts/)).

The current test report also covers 392 test images, and its mIoU of 40.38% is the nearest like-named metric. Treat the numbers as **context, not an apples-to-apples score gap**: this project merges 39 labels into seven, uses YOLO instance segmentation converted to dense class maps, and evaluates with its own rules for unsegmented pixels and ignored labels. The Coralscapes baselines use different semantic-segmentation architectures and their published protocol. A defensible benchmark requires matching the class set, prediction/evaluation protocol, ignored-pixel rules, and checkpoint/data use.

## Overall assessment

The current system is an end-to-end prototype that borrows the papers' broad ideas: reef segmentation, cover summaries, quality flags, and review routing. It is not close to the evidence standard of the core studies yet. The largest gaps are target-region validation, expert-referenced cover error, repeated-survey consistency, actual OOD testing, and uncertainty propagation into the ecological estimates. The project's README explicitly scopes temporal change detection and Lakshadweep-specific validation out of the current implementation.
